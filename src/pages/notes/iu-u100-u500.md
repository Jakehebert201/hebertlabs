An international unit (IU) is a measure of **biological activity**, not a fixed mass. One IU of insulin is not the same mass as one IU of vitamin D or heparin. That means you cannot convert IU to milligrams from memory the way you convert grams to milligrams. You only convert when the problem (or the product labeling) hands you the factor.

## When the problem gives a conversion factor

Treat the factor like any other equality in dimensional analysis. If the label says:

<pre class="formula">1 mg  =  1000 IU</pre>

then either orientation is fair game, depending on which unit you need to cancel:

<pre class="formula">          1000 IU
2.5 mg × ────────  =  2500 IU
            1 mg

             1 mg
5000 IU × ────────  =  5 mg
           1000 IU</pre>

The number 1000 is not a universal constant. It is whatever that product's labeling (or that exam stem) says it is. Read the factor, write it as a fraction, cancel the unit you do not want.

## Insulin strengths: U-100 and U-500

Insulin is labeled by how many units sit in each milliliter:

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| U-100 | 100 units in every 1 mL |
| U-500 | 500 units in every 1 mL |

Those are concentrations, written in a compact pharmacy shorthand. As conversion factors:

<pre class="formula">U-100:   100 units
         ─────────  =  1
           1 mL

U-500:   500 units
         ─────────  =  1
           1 mL</pre>

### Same units, different volumes

Suppose the order is **30 units**.

On U-100:

<pre class="formula">            1 mL
30 units × ────────  =  0.3 mL
           100 units</pre>

On U-500:

<pre class="formula">            1 mL
30 units × ────────  =  0.06 mL
           500 units</pre>

Same patient dose in units. Five times less volume on U-500, because each milliliter is five times as concentrated. That is the whole trap: a U-100 syringe marking is not interchangeable with a U-500 product, and drawing U-500 with U-100 markings overdoses by a factor of five.

### Going the other way

If you know the volume and the product strength, multiply:

<pre class="formula">0.2 mL of U-100 × (100 units / 1 mL)  =  20 units
0.2 mL of U-500 × (500 units / 1 mL)  =  100 units</pre>

## How to choose which factor to use

1. **Mass ↔ IU** only when the stem or the label states the equivalence (for example, "each mcg contains 40 IU"). No stated factor means no conversion.
2. **Units ↔ mL** from the product strength on the vial: U-100 or U-500 (or whatever U-number you are holding).
3. Never mix the two stories. "30 units of U-100" is a volume problem. "30 IU of a vitamin with 1000 IU/mg" is a mass problem. Both say "units," but the conversion chain is different.

  <p>
    <strong>Exam and bedside habit.</strong> Before you cancel anything, name the product strength out loud: "This is U-100, so 100 units per milliliter." If you cannot finish that sentence, you do not yet have the factor you need.
  </p>
</div>
